What a letter means, and how like terms collect (C2.1)

Rules to remember
  • A letter stands for a number you do not know yet. In 3n the letter n could be 2, or 40, or 7.5 — the expression tells you what to do to that number, not what the number is. Units 1 to 3 kept letters out; from here on they are the point.
  • 3n means 3 × n. The multiplication sign is always left out between a number and a letter.
  • A lone letter has a coefficient of 1. Write x, never 1x. So x + 4x = 5x.
  • Only like terms can be collected. Terms are alike when they have the same letter: 2m and 3m collect to 5m, but 2m and 3n do not, and neither do 2m and 3. Plain numbers are alike with other plain numbers.
  • Collecting changes how it is written, not what it is worth. Put m = 4 into 2m + 3m and into 5m: both give 20. That is the check.
  • Letter terms first, plain number last. Write 3x + 5, not 5 + 3x.
