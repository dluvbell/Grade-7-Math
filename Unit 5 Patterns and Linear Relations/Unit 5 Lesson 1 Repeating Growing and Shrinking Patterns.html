Rules to remember
  • Three kinds of pattern. A repeating pattern has a core that comes round again and again. A growing pattern gets larger from one term to the next. A shrinking pattern gets smaller.
  • Growing does not have to mean growing evenly. 3, 6, 9, 12 grows by the same amount each time. 3, 6, 12, 24 grows too, but not by the same amount. Only the first kind has a constant rate.
  • Term number and term value. The term number says which position you are at (1st, 2nd, 3rd …). The term value is what sits in that position.
  • Constant rate = the jump from one term to the next. Subtract any term value from the one after it. In 7, 11, 15, 19 the rate is 4.
  • Initial value = the term value at term number 0. It is the value before the first term, so step back one jump from term 1. In 7, 11, 15 the initial value is 7 − 4 = 3, not 7. This is the trap in this unit.
  • Two ways to say a rule. "Start at 3 and add 4 each time" tells you how to get the next term. "Multiply the term number by 4 and add 3" tells you any term straight away, even the 100th. Both describe the same pattern.
  • A repeating pattern repeats its core. To reach a far position, divide by the core length and use what is left over. With core 4, 7, 1, 9 (length 4), term 50 lands on 50 ÷ 4 = 12 remainder 2, so it is the 2nd item, 7.
  • Comparing two patterns. The one with the greater constant rate climbs faster; the one with the greater initial value starts higher. A pattern can start lower and still overtake.
