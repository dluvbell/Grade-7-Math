Rules to remember
  • A near prediction is just past what you were given. You can walk to it one term at a time, and you can check it by extending the pattern itself.
  • A far prediction is too far to walk. Use the equation: multiply the term number by the rate, then add the initial value. That is what the equation is for.
  • Multiply first, then add. For y = 4x + 7 at term 50: 4 × 50 = 200, then 200 + 7 = 207.
  • Given the value, find the term number by balancing. Put the value in for y, take the initial value off both sides, then divide both sides by the rate. This is the balance method from Unit 4.
  • Always check by substituting back. Put your term number into the original equation. If it does not give the value you started with, something was undone in the wrong order.
  • A gap inside a table can be reached from either side — forward from the row before, backward from the row after. Both must agree. This is interpolation.
  • A point beyond the drawn graph is found from the equation, not by reading the grid. This is extrapolation, and it only works because the rate keeps going.
  • The faster relation does not always arrive first. One that starts far ahead can win even with a smaller rate. Solve both and compare the term numbers.
