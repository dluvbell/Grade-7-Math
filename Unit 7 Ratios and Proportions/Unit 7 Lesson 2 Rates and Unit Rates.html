Rates, unit rates, predicting with them, and comparing two of them

Rules to remember
  • A rate compares two different kinds of thing. Dollars against kilograms, pages against minutes. A ratio compares two of the same kind; a rate does not.
  • A unit rate is what you get for one. $1.25/kg means one kilogram costs $1.25. 30 pages/min means one minute gives 30 pages.
  • Whatever you want one of goes underneath. To find the price for one kilogram, divide the price by the kilograms: $10.00 ÷ 8 kg = $1.25/kg. To find the kilograms for one dollar, divide the other way: 8 kg ÷ $10.00 = 0.8 kg per dollar.
  • Those two are the same deal read from opposite ends. They are not the same number, and swapping them is the most common slip in this lesson. Check the units of your answer: a price ends in dollars, a quantity does not.
  • You can write a rate two ways. In a calculation use the slash form, $1.25/kg. In a sentence use words, “1.25 dollars per kilogram”. They mean exactly the same thing.
  • Different-sized packs cannot be compared directly. $10.00 and $14.40 tell you nothing until you know what each one buys. Bring both down to one unit first, then compare.
  • Smaller is better when you are paying; larger is better when you are making. The cheaper shop has the lower dollars-per-kilogram. The faster printer has the higher pages-per-minute. Decide which kind of rate you have before you decide which number wins.
  • Money always gets two decimal places. $1.5 and $1.50 are the same amount, but only $1.50 is written properly.
