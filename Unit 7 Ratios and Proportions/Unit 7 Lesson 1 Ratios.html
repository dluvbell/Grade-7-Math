Unit 7 Lesson 1 Ratios

Writing, simplifying and comparing ratios, and sharing in a given ratio

Rules to remember
  • A ratio compares two amounts of the same kind. 12 red pencils to 18 blue pencils is written 12 : 18. If the two things are measured in different units, it is a rate, not a ratio.
  • Order matters. 12 : 18 and 18 : 12 answer different questions. Put the number for whatever is named first, first.
  • Part-to-part compares the two parts; part-to-whole compares one part with everyone. With 3 boys and 5 girls, boys to girls is 3 : 5 but boys to all students is 3 : 8. Read which one is being asked for.
  • Equivalent ratios come from multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number. 3 : 5 = 6 : 10 = 12 : 20. Adding the same number to both does not work: 3 : 5 is not the same comparison as 5 : 7.
  • Simplest form means nothing but 1 divides both parts. Divide both by the largest number that goes into both: 18 : 24 = 3 : 4. Dividing by a smaller factor leaves the job half done.
  • To share an amount in a ratio, count the parts first. Sharing $60 in the ratio 2 : 3 means 2 + 3 = 5 equal parts, so one part is $60 ÷ 5 = $12. Then take as many parts as each person is owed: $24 and $36.
  • The numbers in a ratio count parts, not amounts. In 2 : 3 nobody gets $2 or $3 — those are how many shares each person is owed.
  • Check by adding the shares back up. $24 + $36 = $60, so the sharing was done properly.
