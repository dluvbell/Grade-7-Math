Rules to remember
  • Read what the question actually asks for. The part, the new amount, the gap between two amounts and the amount left over are four different answers to the same story. Most wrong options here are one of the others.
  • Percent means hundredths. Fractions, decimals and percents are three ways of writing one amount: 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%.
  • Build the percent from easy pieces. 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 100%. 15% = 10% + 5%. 13% = 10% + 3 × 1%.
  • Decrease by a percent leaves 100% − the percent. A 25% decrease leaves 75%. An increase gives 100% + the percent.
  • Ask what the percent is measured against. This is the trap in almost every Thinking question. A second percent taken "of those" or "of the sale price" works on a different whole than the first one.
  • Two percents cannot be added together unless they are percents of the same amount. 25% off then 10% off is not 35% off.
  • When the whole is missing, work back to it first. If $99 is 110% of the old price, divide by 110 to get 1%, then multiply by 100. Taking 10% off $99 does not undo a 10% increase.
  • A bigger percent is not always a bigger amount. 30% of 100 is less than 20% of 250. Work both out before comparing.
  • Check the direction and the size at the end. After a decrease the answer must be smaller; after an increase, bigger; a part must be smaller than the whole it came from.
  • Money always gets two decimal places, and every percent in this unit lands exactly — nothing needs rounding off.
